Exchange Rate Fluctuation and Economic Growth in Nigeria

An ARDL bounds-testing study of how currency depreciation, inflation, and interest rates affect Nigeria's economic growth, using 44 years of macroeconomic data.

Project overview

This study examined the effect of exchange rate fluctuations on economic growth in Nigeria for the period 1980 to 2024, using annual time series data on six macroeconomic variables: GDP growth rate, exchange rate, inflation rate, lending interest rate, crude oil price, and trade openness.

Business problem

Nigeria's naira has depreciated persistently and, at times, sharply — from roughly ₦0.55 per US dollar in 1980 to ₦1,460 per US dollar in 2024. Policymakers and businesses need to understand whether — and how strongly — this depreciation actually affects economic growth, rather than assuming a relationship.

Research questions

  • Does exchange rate depreciation have a statistically significant effect on Nigeria's GDP growth, in the short run and long run?
  • How do inflation and lending interest rates interact with exchange rate movements to affect growth?
  • Do crude oil prices and trade openness offset or reinforce these effects?
  • How quickly does the Nigerian economy return to equilibrium after a shock?

Data sources

  • World Bank World Development Indicators (WDI)
  • Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) Statistical Bulletin
  • IMF / U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA)

Tools & method

Annual time series data (1980–2024) on GDP growth rate, exchange rate, inflation rate, lending interest rate, crude oil price, and trade openness. Stationarity was tested using the Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) test, followed by ARDL bounds testing (Pesaran, Shin & Smith, 2001) and an Error Correction Model (ECM).

Data preparation

The ADF unit root test showed that exchange rate and lending interest rate were stationary at level (I(0)), while GDP growth, inflation, oil price, and trade openness were stationary after first differencing (I(1)). No variable was integrated of order 2, satisfying the key precondition for using the ARDL approach.

Analysis

The ARDL bounds test produced an F-statistic of 6.52, exceeding the upper critical bound of 4.85 at the 5% significance level — confirming a stable long-run relationship between the variables. The model's R-squared of 0.87 indicates that approximately 87% of the variation in Nigeria's GDP growth is explained by the model. All six diagnostic tests confirmed the model's validity, stability, and reliability.

Key findings

Exchange rate depreciation has a significant negative effect on GDP growth, both in the short run and long run — a 100-unit naira depreciation reduces GDP growth by roughly 0.42 percentage points.
Inflation has a significant negative long-run effect — a 1 percentage point rise in inflation reduces GDP growth by about 0.11 percentage points.
High lending interest rates significantly reduce growth, reflecting a crowding-out effect on private investment.
Crude oil prices have a significant positive effect on growth, confirming Nigeria's continued dependence on oil revenues.
Trade openness has a positive effect on growth, significant at the 10% level.
The error correction term (−0.60) shows that about 60% of any deviation from long-run equilibrium is corrected within a year — a moderately fast speed of adjustment.

Business impact

The findings give policymakers and analysts an evidence-based view of which macroeconomic levers matter most for growth — showing that exchange rate stability, inflation control, and interest rate moderation are not just theoretical concerns but measurable drivers of Nigeria's GDP performance.

Recommendations

Exchange rate stabilization

  • Pursue a transparent, market-consistent exchange rate framework supported by stronger foreign exchange reserves and non-oil export earnings.

Inflation & interest rate management

  • Maintain disciplined monetary policy and pursue progressive interest rate reductions through credit and collateral reform.

Economic diversification

  • Reduce reliance on crude oil revenue through targeted investment in agriculture, manufacturing, and services.

Trade policy

  • Accelerate AfCFTA implementation and reduce non-tariff barriers to broaden non-oil export earnings.

Project outcome

The study confirmed a stable, statistically significant long-run relationship between exchange rate movements and Nigeria's economic growth, and translated that relationship into a concrete set of policy recommendations grounded in the data rather than assumption.
