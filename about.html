From economic models to data models.

How a background in Economics turned into a focus on Data Analytics.

I'm David Adebowale, an Economics graduate from Crawford University, currently based in Lagos, Nigeria. My academic background is built on econometrics, statistics, and macroeconomic theory — subjects that are really about one thing: using data to explain why things happen, and what's likely to happen next.

That habit of mind carried directly into data analytics. My final-year research project used ARDL bounds testing and an Error Correction Model to study how exchange rate movements affect Nigeria's economic growth — which meant cleaning 44 years of macroeconomic data, testing it statistically, and translating the results into recommendations a non-technical reader could act on. That process (clean the data, question it rigorously, explain what it means for a decision) is the same process I now apply with SQL, Python, and Power BI.

Outside my coursework, I've managed financial records and reporting for a 150-member student association as Financial Secretary, and supported inventory operations at Cway — both roles that required me to work carefully with numbers other people depended on.

I'm currently building on that foundation with hands-on data analytics practice — SQL for querying, Python and Pandas for analysis, and Power BI for turning findings into dashboards. I'm looking for an entry-level Data Analyst or Business Analyst role where I can keep developing these skills on real business problems.

Why Economics and Data Analytics fit together

Both disciplines are built on the same core skill: reading numbers carefully enough to make a good decision.

Quantitative reasoning & statistics

Economics trains you to work comfortably with data, probability, and statistical significance — the same foundation data analysis is built on.

Econometrics & research

Building and testing models like ARDL/ECM is directly transferable to structuring an analysis and validating findings before presenting them.

Business & economic decision-making

Economics is ultimately about how decisions get made under constraints — the same lens a Data or Business Analyst brings to a company's numbers.
